I am a Ph.D. candidate at Boston College's Lynch School of Education and Human Development. My research is in education policy and the economics of education. I study how the pathways that high schools offer shape the trajectories students follow into postsecondary education and the workforce. I also use AI to expand the research value of existing administrative and public education data.

My dissertation examines two such pathways and develops new ways to measure them. In career and technical education, I estimate how expanding access to high school health science programs affects students' nursing credential attainment and entry into the nursing workforce. In mathematics, I study how high schools decide who enters advanced courses, and what those placement rules mean for students' later academic performance and access to opportunity.

Before Boston College, I earned a B.A. in Psychology with honors from the University of Texas at Austin, with a certificate in applied statistical modeling and a minor in entrepreneurship.
